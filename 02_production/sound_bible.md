# PUSH — Sound Design Bible

_The single canonical reference for the bed's hum language, movement acoustics, per-tier audio identity, and scene-specific sound direction._

**Status:** Canonical. On any question of how the bed sounds — hum character, pitch/volume modulation, movement acoustics, mixing notes, per-tier audio identity — this document is correct and all other documents defer to it.

**Source of truth for story:** The Screenplay remains the source of truth for story, dialogue, and scene order. This document is the reference for how those scenes sound.

**Related bibles:** The Prop Bible owns physical prop descriptions (construction, materials, states) — including the 42Hz baseline fact, which is cross-referenced here, not duplicated. The Music Bible owns licensed score and diegetic music (the Ludacris thread). This document owns the bed's hum and movement acoustics; any hum-becomes-rhythm interplay with music remains governed here.

## 1. THE COMPLETE HUM LANGUAGE

### 1.1 Baseline Character

Warm. Low. Steady. **42Hz baseline** — deep enough to feel physically, high enough to be clearly audible. Not mechanical in a threatening way — more like the hum of something well-made doing what it was built to do.

The closest sonic reference is the sustained low note of a cello, filtered slightly to remove the organic quality, combined with the undertone of a high-quality HVAC system. It should feel, at baseline, almost _comfortable_. Like white noise with intention. Beneath it, barely audible: the faint rhythm of the tracks rolling.

### 1.3 What the Hum Is NOT

The hum is never aggressive. Never threatening. Never the sound of something malfunctioning. The hum is always the sound of something working exactly as intended. That is what makes it funny, and what makes it moving at the end.

Even at its most pointed (the gas station contempt register), the hum retains the bed's fundamental character of professional patience. It is the difference between a machine idling and a machine that has noticed something it doesn't like.

## 2. PER-TIER AUDIO IDENTITY

### 2.1 The PUSH (Dave's — Base)

- **Sound profile:** Warm low hum, 42Hz baseline. Faint track rhythm on hard floors — individual links passing, drive sprockets engaging. Sheet tensioning is a faint continuous whisper. Pillow node is a slow, patient exhale. The sound of a machine that works well.
- **Character:** Low, present, slightly industrial. The faint industrial edge is Dave's — the base tier's honest mechanical voice.

### 2.2 The PUSH+ (Marcus's — Premium)

- **Sound profile:** Same frequency architecture as the PUSH, refined. Lower, smoother motor hum — **you feel it more than hear it**. Dampened track roll. Pneumatic exhale is barely there — a breath, not a mechanism. Sheet tensioning is nearly inaudible. Panel and strut movement is solid, muted, damped — the "door closing" feel. Nothing rattles. Nothing clicks. It just moves. The sound of a machine engineered to disappear.
- **Character:** Smoother, more refined. The difference between a Camry door and an LS door. You feel it without being able to articulate it.

### 2.3 The Tier Contrast (Canonical)

In the office: **Dave's bed hums. Marcus's bed purrs.** Same frequency range, different texture entirely. The audience may not consciously register why one sounds "better" — but they feel it.

### 2.4 The Dual-Hum Chord

When both beds are in frame and idling, their hums are slightly different — not just in pitch and sync, but in _quality_. Dave's has a faint industrial edge. Marcus's PUSH+ is smoother, more refined. The chord they create together is the sound of two men who made the same bad decision at wildly different price points.

The two hums are slightly out of sync — a deliberate detail. The chord is the sound of the film's central joke.

## 3. MOVEMENT ACOUSTICS

### 3.1 The Tracks

The tracks are nearly silent. The motor hum is the primary audio signal of movement. Beneath the hum, barely audible: the faint rhythm of the tracks rolling — individual links passing, drive sprockets engaging on hard floors.

The bed rolls smoothly and continuously — like heavy furniture on felt pads, pushed by someone who knows where they're going and has all day to get there. No lurching, no stepping, no visible effort.

### 3.2 The Self-Making Sounds

- **Sheet tensioning:** A faint, continuous whisper beneath the motor hum — the sound of something being drawn very carefully across a surface. The bar moves slowly, deliberately; wrinkles smooth one at a time. The full pass takes several minutes. It is not in a hurry.
- **Pillow node (PUSH):** One soft sound, extended — less a puff than a quiet, patient exhale. The node works gradually: slow sustained inflation, brief hold, calibrated deflation. The whole cycle takes longer than it should. That is the point.
- **Pillow node (PUSH+):** Barely there — a breath, not a mechanism.
- **Panel and strut movement (PUSH+):** Solid, muted, damped — the "door closing" feel. Nothing rattles. Nothing clicks. It just moves.

### 3.3 The Shower Curtain Nudge (Scene 5)

The bed stays at the bathroom doorway — it cannot physically reach the curtain. The "nudge" is communicated entirely through the motor hum shifting pitch and Dave's reaction. The curtain moves from Dave's own movement, not from contact with the bed. This is funnier — the bed enforces its will without being visible.

## 4. SCENE-SPECIFIC SOUND DIRECTION

### 4.1 The Drive-Through Escalation (Scene 10, Beat 1)

The bed's hum rises — the same escalating pitch from the bathroom. Urgency: _we need to go_. Dave grips the steering wheel. Pulls away. The joke is complete the moment he drives away.

### 4.2 The Gas Station Warning Hum (Scene 10, Beat 3)

The warning hum is distinct from the drive-through escalation. The drive-through hum was urgency — _we need to go_. The gas station hum is **contempt** — _we are not stopping for this_.

- **Pitch:** Slightly higher than baseline, pointed.
- **Duration:** Approximately two seconds the first time. If Dave continues slowing, it returns — slightly more pointed, slightly shorter.
- **Quality:** A quality of attention being withdrawn rather than applied. It should read as: _I see what you're thinking. Don't._
- **Mixing:** The audience should feel the first hum slightly before they consciously register what Dave is looking at. The hum and the gas gauge land at the same moment.
- **What it is NOT:** Not alarm. Not concern for Dave's wellbeing. Not the sound of a machine noticing a problem. It is the sound of a machine that has a schedule and does not appreciate detours.

### 4.3 The 4:58pm Exhale (Scene 17)

The single exhale note when Dave finishes the Hendricks report. This is the emotional peak of the bed's arc.

- **Sound:** One single, small, lower-pitched exhale note. Brief. Then back to idle.
- **Mixing:** Mixed so that Dave registers it. The audience should feel it as a moment of genuine communication between two beings who have spent a very long day together.
- **Performance:** Dave registers it. That's all. No words, no gesture toward the bed. The moment passes. This is the closest thing to communication between them all day, and it happens without either of them making anything of it.

### 4.4 The End-Credits Fade

The commute home is a single wide shot — the bed rolling down a city sidewalk alone, vertical, unhurried, in late afternoon light. The hum fades as it rounds the corner. The last sound of the film. No music under the credits — just the hum, fading. (Music decisions are governed by the Music Bible; the hum's presence under everything is governed here.)
