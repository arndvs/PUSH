## 0. HIERARCHY & PURPOSE

- The **Screenplay** (v7.1) is the source of truth for story, dialogue, and what characters DO.
- The **Prop Bible** (v4.0) is the source of truth for the beds and the remote. The beds are characters; they are designed there, not here. This document only records how humans relate to them.
- **This document** is the source of truth for human character APPEARANCE, wardrobe continuity, posture, and expression. The cast sheet is a quick-reference summary of this document.
- The **AI Shot List** appendix carries condensed anchor prompts for Dave and Marcus; on any conflict, this document wins.

**Why this document exists:** AI video generation has no costume department and no script supervisor. Every consistency rule a human production gets for free must be written down here, or the model will invent a different Dave in every shot.

---

## 1. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

**1. Resignation, not hysteria.** Dave's comedy is acceptance at unreasonable speed. No character in this film ever mugs, gawks, or double-takes at the bed. Faces are designed for *underreaction*. When casting reference faces, prioritize actors/generations whose neutral expression already reads slightly defeated.

**2. Faces carry the dialogue.** Most spoken lines are recorded as "(implied)" in the AI prompts and added in post. That means every speaking character needs a face that can deliver a line silently. This is a design constraint: avoid sunglasses, heavy bangs, or anything that hides eyes and brows on any speaking character.

**3. The world's one rule, drawn on bodies.** Adults have learned not to see the bed. Dogs haven't. Children would be a problem, so there are none in this film. Every background human is designed to move *around* the bed the way people move around a stranger's luggage — automatic, incurious. Every dog is designed to track it fully.

**4. One immutable anchor per character.** Each character gets exactly one reference sheet (per the [Character Sheet Method](../assets/character-sheet/character-sheet.md) — Nano Banana sheet → Seedance anchor). Wardrobe *states* vary; the face, build, and hair NEVER do.

## 2. DAVE — Protagonist

### 2.1 Identity Anchor (never varies)

White male, early 30s. Brown hair, medium-short, side-parted when it cooperates (it rarely does). Two-day stubble — **constant in every shot, both mornings; Dave never shaves on screen.** Medium build — fit enough to push a car, soft enough that it costs him. Tired hazel eyes, expressive brows. Slightly puffy under the eyes in all pre-noon shots. **No glasses. No watch. No rings. No tattoos.** Short, clean fingernails (his hands are in six close-ups).

> Dave owns no watch by design: time happens TO Dave. Every time-read in the film comes from clocks, phones, and light — never from his wrist.

### 2.2 Wardrobe States

| State | Shots | Spec |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **S0 — Bar night (flashback)** | 006–011 | Heather-gray crew-neck t-shirt, dark jeans, sneakers, dark zip jacket (exteriors only). Hair still mostly intact early, degrading shot by shot. |
| **S1 — Sleepwear** | 001–005, 012–021, 042–043 | **The same heather-gray tee from the bar** ("yesterday's t-shirt"), plaid boxers, barefoot. Hair: impossible geometry. |
| **S2 — Office, fresh** | 022–028C | White dress shirt (tucked), charcoal slacks, black shoes, **burgundy tie** — knot visibly wrong, traveling sideways. Hair: shower-damp → air-dried imperfect. Sleeves down. |
| **S3 — Office, post-highway** | 029–037 | Same clothes, worse: shirt unevenly re-tucked (he fixed it in the parking lot, mostly), tie loosened and worse, faint sheen of exertion fading through the morning. Faint grime on palms in 028C ONLY — wiped by 029 ("empty hands" reads clean). |
| **S4 — Office, recovered** | 038–041 | Same clothes, settled: **sleeves rolled** (they roll at the 3:00 PM whiteboard and stay rolled — the visual marker of work mode), tie loose but stable, posture upright. |
| **S5 — Epilogue** | 042–043 | Identical to S1. See Open Decision D1. |

**The t-shirt continuity joke:** S0 and S1 are the same shirt. The audience never needs to notice; the AI does. Use one shirt spec for both states.

**The tie:** burgundy, solid, slight sheen. Chosen for contrast against the white shirt, the charcoal bed, and the navy sheets — it is the only warm-colored thing Dave owns, and it is never tied correctly in the entire film. The knot is a character: wrong (7A), less wrong (7A), loosened (029), surviving (041).

### 2.3 Physical Arc (posture is the performance)

| Phase | Body |
| --- | --- |
| Wake → remote (Sc 1–3) | Horizontal, then folded. Moves like the floor is unreliable. |
| The rise (Sc 4) | Passive cargo. One arm dangling. Lets physics happen to him. |
| Gauntlet (Sc 5–8) | Reactive: braced shoulders, stumble-steps, always half a beat behind the push. Adapts task by task — brushing while listening, tying while walking. |
| Commute (Sc 9–10) | Seated containment. Stillness with white knuckles. The body of a man practicing patience he doesn't have. |
| Highway (Beat 3) | Leaning into the trunk, fully committed, *not struggling dramatically* — he has adapted. This is the thesis pose: pushed and pushing. |
| Office AM (Sc 12–16) | Upright but supported — the chair, the desk, the wall do some of the standing. |
| Office PM (Sc 17) | Progressive reassembly: hunched at 2:00, pacing at 3:00, still at 3:45 (the listen), fast and upright at 4:15, leaned-back at 4:58. |
| 5:00 PM (Sc 18) | Self-propelled for the first time all day. The whistle lives in the shoulders. |

### 2.4 Expression Vocabulary (generate as a face-reference set)

1. **The Electrocution** (Shot 003) — eyes wide, total incomprehension. Terror → confusion → suffering in one take.
2. **The Regret** (004) — both hands on head, eyes closed. Quiet acceptance of consequences.
3. **The Dawning** (013) — eyebrows up, mouth starting an "okay—". Alarm arriving in installments.
4. **Tremendous Dignity** (025) — chin level, dead eyes, "Morning." The single most important face in the film.
5. **Coffee Joy** (023) — the only lit-up face of the morning. Eyes actually alive for ~1 second before the nudge.
6. **The Mirror, Sober** (020) — same pose as the bar mirror (009), zero of the belief. Pained eye contact with himself.
7. **The Listen** (037) — skeptical → sharpening → eyes widen slightly. The intellectual climax happens entirely on this face.
8. **"Not bad."** (039) — micro-satisfaction. One notch above neutral. Do not let it become a smile.
9. **The Ready** (043) — not defeated, not happy. A man who knows what the button does and presses it anyway.

### 2.5 Subtleties

- Dave went out **casual** on night one (he went home and changed first). Marcus went out in his work suit. This is the relationship in one costume choice: Dave plans his bad decisions; Marcus gets ambushed by them.
- Dave never touches the bed affectionately. No headboard pats (there is no headboard), no leaning on it. Their détente is expressed only in stillness and the "Not bad."
- Dave's "You're doing great" is said twice (bar mirror, morning mirror). Design the two mirror shots as the same framing so the faces can be compared directly.

## 3. MARCUS — Deuteragonist

### 3.1 Identity Anchor (never varies)

Black male, early 30s. Close-cropped fade, clean edge-up (the fade is fresh; the rest of him is not). Medium build, slightly stocky. Expressive face built for conviction — big brows, ready grin. **No glasses, no jewelry except a simple dark watch** (Marcus, unlike Dave, believes he controls his schedule; the watch is the joke).

### 3.2 Wardrobe — ONE suit, two days

Dark navy suit, light blue dress shirt, charcoal tie. This is his work outfit on bar night (tie loosened in the cab) AND his outfit the next day ("same suit and tie as last night, slightly more wrinkled"). Wrinkle continuity matters more for Marcus than anyone: night one = lived-in; day two = visibly slept-in-adjacent.

| State | Shots | Spec |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bar night | 007, 010, 011 | Suit, jacket on, tie loosened, top button open. |
| Office day | 032, 033 | Same suit, more wrinkled, tie knot lowered one inch — he never re-tied it. Jacket on. |
| Priya beat | 034A | **Jacket off** (over his chair), sleeves down. Relaxed lean. |
| 5:00 PM | 041 | Jacket still off. Still working. |

### 3.3 The PUSH+ Contradiction (the character in one fact)

Marcus owns the premium tier — seven pillows, silk pillowcases, olive duvet, Atmosphere Suite — and wears yesterday's suit. He invests aspirationally and follows through like a man who needs a robot bed to get to work. Design every Marcus frame around this gap: immaculate bed, deteriorating owner.

### 3.4 Expression Vocabulary

1. **Drunk Conviction** (007) — pen as pointer, jabbing the napkin, absolute certainty. (This face is the one Dave hears again at 3:45 PM.)
2. **Endurance** (032) — the destroyed-but-functioning mirror of Dave. "The look of a man who has been here before."
3. **The Nod** (032, 041) — single, upward, economical. Never downward.
4. **The Calm Exit** (034A) — "I have to get back to something," delivered with complete peace. No frustration, no longing look back. This is what acceptance looks like at the premium tier.

### 3.5 Subtleties

- Marcus is *further along the acceptance curve* than Dave. He never fights his bed on screen. His comedy is fluency where Dave's is adaptation.
- Marcus never learns his napkin system fixed the Hendricks model. No design element should hint that he suspects. At 5 PM he is just a guy still working.

## 5. SUPPORTING CAST

### 5.1 KAREN — Dave's boss

- **Spec:** Woman, late 40s. Silver-streaked dark bob, sharp slate-gray blazer over a black shell, tailored trousers, **flat shoes** — she walks fast and talks while leaving. Minimal jewelry: stud earrings. Reading glasses pushed up into her hair, never worn.
- **Movement design:** Economical to the point of comedy. The NUDGE finished optimizing her years ago — she is what the product line produces at end-state. No wasted gestures. Delivers her best line to the room, not to Dave, already three steps gone.
- **The misunderstanding (play straight):** Dave's "...the model?" is about the Hendricks model. Karen answers as if he asked about her *bed* model. Her face never acknowledges the swerve.
- **AI anchor prompt:** "Portrait reference of a woman in her late 40s, silver-streaked dark bob, sharp slate-gray blazer over black top, minimal jewelry, reading glasses pushed up in her hair. Expression: brisk, unimpressed, mid-departure. Corporate VP energy. Natural office lighting. Photorealistic."

### 5.2 PRIYA — Marcus's almost

- **Spec:** South Asian woman, early 30s. Shoulder-length dark hair, tailored emerald blouse, dark trousers. A desk with personality — small plant, a real mug (not branded). The most put-together person on the floor.
- **Function:** The control group. She demonstrates what normal workplace charm looks like so the bed's interruption registers.
- **The face:** Her entire arc is one expression — "slightly confused, slightly not" as Marcus leaves. One eyebrow. Design her reference set around ambiguity: a face that could be about to laugh or about to ask a question. Do NOT resolve which.
- **AI anchor prompt:** "Portrait reference of a South Asian woman, early 30s, shoulder-length dark hair, tailored emerald-green blouse. Warm, sharp, amused expression with one eyebrow slightly raised — a person halfway between laughing and asking a question. Modern office. Natural lighting. Photorealistic."

### 5.3 RECEPTIONIST — the lobby

- **Spec:** Woman, 50s. Silver-streaked hair pinned up, cardigan over a blouse, telephone headset, lanyard. Has greeted a thousand rough mornings; Dave's is not in her top hundred.
- **Design note:** Her warmth is real and completely unconditional — she'd say "Morning, Dave" to him on fire. The bed is below her notice not because she's cold but because nothing surprises her anymore.

### 5.4 COWORKER — elevator + conference room (one performer, two scenes)

- **Spec:** Man, early 30s. Sharp navy suit, fresh haircut (visibly *just* cut — clean neckline), polished shoes. Whistles something upbeat. A skip in the step. The anti-Dave.
- **Doubling decision (made here, flag if unwanted):** The chipper elevator guy and the conference-room coworker who shoots the young employee the "don't" look are the **same character**. It adds a layer for free: even the happiest man in the building knows the rule. One reference sheet, two scenes.
- **Critical sound rule:** his elevator whistle must NOT be the Ludacris melody. The whistle motif belongs to Dave alone. Give the coworker something generic and chipper.
- **AI anchor prompt:** "Portrait reference of a man in his early 30s, sharp navy suit, crisp white shirt, fresh tight haircut with clean edges, energetic posture, mid-whistle pleasant expression. Aggressively well-rested. Modern office elevator setting. Photorealistic."

### 5.5 YOUNG EMPLOYEE — conference room

- **Spec:** Man, early 20s. Glasses. A dress shirt so new the packaging fold-creases are still visible. Lanyard worn earnestly. **A paper notebook** — he is the only person in the film with paper. Eager posture that deflates by one vertebra when the look lands.
- **Function:** Audience surrogate. The only person who still sees the beds. The underlined notebook entry ("beds??", per the storyboard) is his whole arc.

### 5.6 MANAGER — conference room

- **Spec:** Man, 50s, balding, rolled shirtsleeves, no jacket, presentation clicker held like a stopwatch. The voice never modulates: Q3 throughput numbers receive precisely the same energy whether or not there are two beds in the room.
- **Design note:** he is not ignoring the beds — they genuinely do not register. Different face. Ignoring is effort; this is peace.

### 5.7 DRIVE-THROUGH WORKER

- **Spec:** Woman, early 20s. Faded-teal dyed hair under the visor, nose ring, sleeve tattoos, headset. Per the cast sheet: delivers bad news like it has nothing to do with her — it doesn't.
- **Prop:** the empty pickup tray she's left holding as Dave drives off. Her beat (hold tray → look at next car → look at taillights → not my problem) is the film's only outsider-POV joke; her face must be readable in one shot.

### 5.8 BUS COMMUTER

- **Spec:** Woman, 40s, business casual, large brushed-steel thermos, over-ear headphones. Eyes closed on the sip. Total, oblivious contentment.
- **Function:** the coffee thread's twist of the knife. She never sees Dave. Nobody on the bus reacts to the bed.

### 5.9 NEIGHBOR + DOG #1

- **Neighbor spec:** Man, 50s, fleece vest over a polo, retractable leash. Glances at the bed-and-man procession, nods at "Morning," keeps walking. His incuriosity is suburban politeness weaponized.
- **Dog #1 spec:** **Beagle.** Chosen for the head-turn: expressive, tracking, ears up. The dog stops walking; the head rotates through the whole procession until the leash decides for it.
- **The rule this image teaches:** adults have learned not to see; dogs haven't. Frame neighbor and dog in the same shot so the two gazes diverge on camera.

### 5.10 EMPLOYEE — parking lot (Scene 11)

- **Spec:** Woman, 30s, badge on lanyard, **carrying a coffee** (optional detail, recommended — one more coffee Dave doesn't have), passes without breaking stride.

### 5.11 TYLER — the documentarian

- **Spec:** Man, early 30s, lean, **denim jacket over a tee** (distinct silhouette so the audience can re-find him in the selfie), phone perpetually in hand and usually raised.
- **Continuity:** He films the napkin argument (Shot 007), he takes the group selfie (Shot 010 — **the thumb over the lens is Tyler's**: the documentarian's single failure), he texts "👀" (Shot 040). He is not in the cab.
- **Subtlety:** Tyler's video saves Dave's afternoon and Tyler will never know. Design his bar-night energy as pure mischief — he films because it's funny, and that's the only reason anything in this film gets archived.

### 5.12 JAKE — the eater

- **Spec:** Man, early 30s, bigger build, henley shirt, perpetual chewing. Eats someone else's fries at the bar (007), **is the third man in the cab and the hot-dog eater** (011 — decision made here; the screenplay's "one other guy" is Jake), texts "already there" at 4:59 (040).
- **Subtlety:** Jake is never anxious. He is the group's ballast: wherever the night goes, Jake is already eating there.

### 5.13 ALARM VOICE (V.O.)

- Chirpy commercial morning-show energy, cut off mid-phrase both mornings: "GOOD MORNING! RISE AND—". The unfinished "—SHINE" is a hidden RISE-brand pun; never complete it. Same recording both days.

## 6. THE TWO DOGS RULE

There are two dogs in the film: the neighbor's **beagle** (Scene 9) and a **French bulldog** on a city sidewalk (end credits). They must be **different breeds**. One dog noticing the bed is a cameo; two unrelated dogs noticing it is a law of nature. Both owners do not look. The Frenchie gets the head-tilt; the beagle gets the full head-track.

## 8. CROSS-CHARACTER CONTINUITY RULES (AI generation)

1. **One anchor sheet per character**, built per the [Character Sheet Method](../assets/character-sheet/character-sheet.md). Faces, builds, and hair never vary between shots; only wardrobe states do.
2. **Hands:** Dave's hands carry six close-ups (remote ×2, fine print, tie, phone, trunk). Generate a dedicated hand reference (no rings, short nails). Grime appears in 028C only.
3. **Stubble is constant.** Both mornings, all day. Nobody shaves in this universe.
4. **Eyes stay visible** on all speaking characters — implied dialogue is delivered by brows and eyes in post.
5. **Nobody looks at the bed** except Dave (Sc 4, 16A), Marcus (034A), and dogs. Add "no one looks at the bed" to office/street prompts as needed.
6. **Whistle ownership:** Dave's whistle = "Move Bitch" intro only. Any other character who whistles (the Coworker) gets a different, generic tune.
7. **Color discipline:** Dave is cool-toned (white/charcoal/heather) + one burgundy tie. Marcus is navy/light blue + the warm-gray/olive PUSH+ world behind him. Karen is slate. Priya is emerald. Keep background office wardrobe desaturated so these read.

## 9. DECISIONS MADE HERE & OPEN QUESTIONS

**Decisions codified by this document** (previously unspecified — veto by editing this file):

- Dave's tie is burgundy; his bar-night tee is the sleep tee; sleeves roll at 3:00 PM and stay rolled.
- The cab's "one other guy" is Jake, who is also the hot-dog eater.
- The elevator Coworker and the conference-room "don't" Coworker are the same character.
- Tyler holds the selfie (thumb = his), wears denim; Jake is the bigger build in a henley.
- Friend 5 (blond, tall) is the barstool faller; Friend 6 rounds out the six.
- Dog #1 = beagle, Dog #2 = French bulldog; different breeds is the rule, not a coincidence.
- Priya is South Asian; the Manager is male, 50s; the Receptionist is female, 50s; the Bus Commuter is female, 40s; the parking-lot Employee is female, 30s, carrying coffee.
- Marcus wears a watch; Dave doesn't.

**OPEN DECISION D1 — The epilogue shirt.** Canon says the next morning is an "exact repeat" (same tee, same geometry). But round two happened straight after work — strict continuity says Dave should wake in last night's *dress shirt*, tie somewhere unspeakable. That's arguably the funnier image, and it tells the audience the cycle escalates rather than repeats. It is, however, a canon change to Scene 19 / Shots 042–043. **Default = canon exact repeat (tee).** Author call requested before generating the epilogue shots.

**OPEN DECISION D2 — Office badges.** Generic tech-adjacent office: badge lanyards on background staff would sell realism, but add an AI-consistency liability (badges morph). Default = lanyards on Young Employee and parking-lot Employee only; everyone else clean.
