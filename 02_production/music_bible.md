## 1. THE SOUNDTRACK REGISTRY

Every track in both videos and what it is used for.

| Track | Artist | Video(s) | Usage | Fidelity |
| ----- | ------ | -------- | ----- | -------- |
| "Move Bitch" | Ludacris | PUSH | Radio intro whistle (Scene 10) + Dave's diegetic whistle (Scenes 10, 18) | Intro whistle only. Instrumental. No lyrics. |
| "Stand Up" | Ludacris ft. Shawnna | RISE Move teaser | Full track, continuous under the entire video, edit cut to the beat | Full track. Publishing and master cleared. |

### 1.1 "Move Bitch" (Ludacris) — PUSH

- **Usage:** The radio plays the intro whistle in Scene 10 (car). Dave picks it up and whistles along. The whistle returns at 5PM (Scene 18) — same phrase, complete this time.
- **Fidelity:** Intro whistle only. Instrumental only. No lyrics are ever heard.
- **Diegetic status:** The radio is diegetic. Dave's whistle is diegetic. The song was already playing when the car turned on — Dave didn't choose it.
- **License note:** License the intro instrumental only.

### 1.2 "Stand Up" (Ludacris ft. Shawnna) — RISE Move Teaser

- **Usage:** Opens cold over black — the beat drops before anything is seen. Runs continuously under the entire video. The edit is cut to the track — every shot change lands on a beat. No voiceover competes with it. Text on screen reads between bars. The track fades only for Dr. Voss's quote — which lands in the pocket of silence between the final chorus and the outro. Returns for the RISE Move wordmark, then fades out clean.
- **Thesis:** _"When I move you move, just like that."_ This is the video's entire thesis in one line. When the Move moves, you move. Just like that. The track is not ironic. It is not a winking choice. It is the most accurate description of the product that has ever been recorded.
- **The track does not comment on the product. The track IS the product's energy.** Confident. Forward. Synchronized.

## 3. THE BOOKEND SEMANTICS

The canonical description of the two whistles. Downstream documents defer instead of restate.

### 3.1 The First Whistle (Scene 10 — Car)

- **Found.** Unconscious. The radio was already playing it when the car turned on — Dave didn't choose it.
- **Interrupted mid-phrase** by the fuel gauge discovery. Dave kills the radio. Silence. The bed hums.
- The whistle stopping mid-phrase is the beat — hold the silence after the radio cuts.

### 3.2 The Second Whistle (Scene 18 — 5 PM)

- **Earned.** Unhurried. Complete. The same phrase, resolved.
- The morning's interruption is over. No accompaniment. Just a man whistling on his way to round two.
- The whistle is the bookend: the morning interrupted, the afternoon earned.

### 3.3 The Private Joke

No lyrics are ever heard. Instrumental whistle only. The audience who recognizes the song gets a private joke about a man being pushed through his day whistling a song about making people move.

## 4. THE DIEGETIC RULES

- **Which music is diegetic:** The radio (Scene 10). Dave's whistle (Scenes 10, 18).
- **Which music is not:** No underscore under the opening through alarm — silence only. The flashback montage has "something that sounds like a good night" — upbeat, slightly blurry, nostalgic. Fades out when we return to morning.
- **The Gauntlet:** Consider the bed's hum as the rhythm track. Build percussion around it rather than layering over it. The hum should be audible under everything. _(Hum governance: Sound Design Bible.)_
- **No music under the end credits.** Just the hum, fading. This is a binding editorial product decision.
- **No title card** for the whistle. The song is never named in the film.

## 5. THE CROSS-VIDEO PREMISE

**The universe runs on Ludacris.**

Both videos feature Ludacris. Both are about movement. "Move Bitch" is Dave's song — found on the radio, whistled by a man being pushed through his day. "Stand Up" is the product's song — the bed's energy, the brand promise. The connection is never stated. The audience who watches both pieces feels it.
